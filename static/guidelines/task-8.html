Basic Surgical Actions-10

The ten categories below describe basic surgical actions, with a definition and visual example for each category.

  • Aspiration
  • Clipping
  • Coagulation
  • Dissection
  • Knot-tying
  • Needle Grasping
  • Needle Puncture
  • Packaging
  • Suture Pulling
  • Tissue Retraction

Aspiration

Removal of fluid or tissue debris from the operative field using suction.

Clipping

Placement of clips or clamps to stop bleeding or occlude a vessel or tissue.

Coagulation

Use of heat to coagulate proteins, achieve hemostasis, or seal tissue.

Dissection

Separation or cutting of tissue to reveal underlying anatomy.

Knot-tying

Formation and tightening of suture loops into a knot that secures tissue.

Needle Grasping

Approach toward a needle followed by grasping it; the movement into the grasp is emphasized.

Needle Puncture

Advancement of a needle into tissue with the intended entry angle and depth.

Packaging

Placement of tissue or other material into a sterile bag to contain contamination.

Suture Pulling

Pulling a needle or thread to draw suture through tissue after needle passage.

Tissue Retraction

Holding or moving tissue or an organ aside to expose the operative area.
